# AI Governance Toolkit

> **Illustrative data.** The register describes a fictional organisation. System names, owners, scores and dates are examples, not real data.

## Purpose
This toolkit demonstrates a practical approach to maintaining visibility and control over AI systems across an organisation, covering inventory, risk classification, ownership, and review cycles, mapped to NIST AI RMF principles and ISO/IEC 42001 concepts. Built to reflect how AI governance can move from policy language into an operational, auditable process.

## Contents
- **AI_System_Register.csv**: Example inventory of AI systems with ownership, likelihood and impact scores, risk classification, data sensitivity, approval status, and review dates (last review and next review due).
- **AI_Risk_Scoring_Methodology.md**: A 5x5 likelihood/impact scoring approach for AI-specific risks (bias, hallucination, data leakage, model drift, explainability). Rating bands match my ISO 27001 SmartDesk assessment.
- **AI_Policy_Template.md**: A concise AI use policy covering governance structure, approval workflow, human oversight, exceptions, incident reporting and third-party AI.
- **Framework_Mapping.md**: Maps toolkit elements to NIST AI RMF functions and ISO/IEC 42001 clauses.
- **AI_Governance_Workflow.png**: Visual overview of the AI governance lifecycle: intake, risk assessment, approval, and monitoring.

## Context
Built to explore how organisations can operationalise AI governance in practice, particularly relevant as AI adoption accelerates faster than most governance structures can keep pace with. This reflects an interest in bridging technical AI literacy with structured, auditable governance discipline.
